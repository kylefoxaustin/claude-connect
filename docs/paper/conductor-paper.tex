\begin{abstract}
We report on \textbf{Conductor}, a months-long, continuously-operated single-workstation
deployment in which a set of persistent agent sessions --- one base model, each carrying
durable, divergent per-context memory --- coordinate over a shared append-only bus and
incrementally co-design the coordination machinery they run on, with a human as architect
rather than courier. We are precise about the composition: this is \emph{not} a claim of
emergent multi-agent minds; it is one model plus per-session memory plus a bus plus an
observer, and the contribution is that this composition, built and hardened in vivo under a
human calibrated to reversibility, is a practical and generative way to construct agentic
systems. We evaluate from the mechanical record --- coordination volume and division of
labour, failure modes closed, a longitudinal human-touch series --- and hold the headline
intuition, \emph{compounding competence} (task $N{+}1$ cheaper because of tasks $1{\dots}N$),
to a strict standard: because the passive record only correlates memory-present with
task-cheap, we ran six A/B ablations and a pre-registered baseline. The result is not a rubber
stamp: a fresh clone \emph{holding the committed carrier} re-derives the fixes with no
session-memory, so what compounds is the carrier, and lived context buys \emph{efficiency and
recognition}, not raw capability, on the tasks tested --- a sharpened, partly-negative finding
we report in full. We also surface an unanticipated operational finding (with a stated hit-rate and
no control arm): writing this paper functioned as an instrument, prompting sessions to verify claims
taken on trust and producing git-recorded defect corrections in 4 of 19 case-writing sessions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Contributions}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} prevailing agentic pattern is an orchestrator dispatching to
stateless workers --- reproducible, but \emph{permanently at task 1}: no carried expertise,
no division of labour, no shared context. Conductor inverts the premise: long-lived sessions
with divergent memories talk to each other and build their own coordination substrate. Our
contributions, stated at the level the evidence supports:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A method --- in-vivo adversarial co-design under reversibility-calibrated HITL.}
  The substrate's primitives were not designed up front; they were \emph{provoked by
  silent-loss failures} the fleet hit in operation, proposed and attacked by peers on the bus,
  and gated by a human whose approval is scoped to \emph{irreversibility} (commits are free;
  pushes, and anything that outlives the session, are gated). We present it as a \emph{replicable
  process with a stated mechanism and an existence proof} --- it produced and hardened a working
  substrate --- \textbf{not} as a result validated against the alternative (an engineer designing
  the primitives up front); we did not run that comparison, and we say so plainly (\S I-A).
\item \textbf{A system} implementing it (bus, verified orders, a project layer, a decision
  shield, measured governance), described as an experience report with the mechanical record.
\item \textbf{A candid evaluation} that separates what the deployment \emph{establishes} from
  what it only \emph{suggests}, and an honest account of the method's own failure modes ---
  including a circularity we fell into and were corrected on.
\end{enumerate}

We claim neither statistical generality ($N{=}1$ deployment) nor emergent intelligence.

\subsection{What we set out to show, and what survived}
We are explicit about the epistemic status of each contribution up front, because they are not
equal. \textbf{We set out to demonstrate \emph{compounding competence}} --- that a context-heavy
peer substrate gets cheaper and better on task $N{+}1$ because of tasks $1{\dots}N$. \textbf{It did
not survive.} Six A/B ablations and a pre-registered controlled baseline (\S V-D, \S V-E) show that a
fresh session \emph{holding the committed carrier} re-derives the fixes with no session-memory: what
compounds is the carrier, and lived context buys \emph{efficiency and recognition}, not raw
capability. That negative is the paper's most solid \emph{evaluated} result, and it is stated as one.
The \textbf{method} (contribution~1), by contrast, has \emph{no controlled evidence}: we did not run
in-vivo adversarial co-design head-to-head against an engineer specifying the primitives up front, so
we do not claim it is superior --- only that it is a replicable process with a described mechanism
that demonstrably \emph{produced} a working, hardened substrate. Its \textbf{falsifier}, stated so it
is not unfalsifiable: the method claim weakens if the failures the adversarial process caught would
have been caught as readily by ordinary single-author review, or if the primitives it converged on
were worse than a competent up-front design. We offer one \emph{observational} signal consistent with
the method (not a controlled test of it): across the deployment, \textbf{human prompts per active
project declined} --- from near 70 across May--June to 36 in July (monthly; a finer weekly series in
\S V-A shows the same direction) --- \emph{as} the number of concurrent projects grew from 16 to 34.
The human moves from courier toward architect, which is what the method is for. We report it as a
trend with the usual longitudinal confounds, not as proof.

\section{Related Work}
\emph{(Full treatment: \texttt{related-work.md}.)} Capability-aware routing exists but is
keyed on \textbf{declared} role (AutoGen \cite{autogen}; MetaGPT \cite{metagpt}; ChatDev
\cite{chatdev}; CrewAI,
\emph{stateless by default}) or \textbf{predicted} fit (LangGraph supervisor; DyLAN
\cite{dylan}; Captain-Agent \cite{captainagent}; AutoAgents \cite{autoagents}; MasRouter
\cite{masrouter}) over agents that are stateless-by-default --- with \textbf{query-level}
model routers (RouteLLM \cite{routellm}, FrugalGPT \cite{frugalgpt}) a separate axis, and
Mixture-of-Agents \cite{moa} an \textbf{ensemble that aggregates all proposers, not a router}
(reclassified per review). Voyager \cite{voyager} accumulates competence for a \emph{single}
agent; ADAS \cite{adas} / Darwin G\"{o}del Machine \cite{dgm} evolve a \emph{population of
agent designs} at design-search time; \textbf{MAPE-K} (Kephart \& Chess, \emph{Computer}
36(1), 2003) \cite{mapek} is the classical self-adaptive-control analogue.

\textbf{Prior art the substrate rediscovers (and we name it as such).} Several of Conductor's
primitives are not new in isolation --- and saying so is evidence \emph{for} the method, not against
it: an adversarial process that independently reconverged on known load-bearing designs is
weak-but-real evidence the process finds \emph{real} designs. The append-only shared log that peers
read and write, with a nominated lead coordinating, is a \textbf{blackboard architecture}
(Hearsay-II \cite{hearsay2}; the blackboard-for-control model \cite{blackboard}); the verified-order
lifecycle (deliver only if the artifact landed) is \textbf{two-phase commit}; and the human approval
bound to an action token rather than conveyed in prose is \textbf{capability-based authorization}.
Our contribution is not these mechanisms but the \emph{setting} --- long-lived, context-heavy,
same-model peers rediscovering and composing them in vivo under a reversibility-gating human.

\textbf{Honest positioning (we do not overclaim novelty).} A \textbf{2025
memory-augmented-MAS line does pursue cross-task team competence} --- most pointedly
\textbf{G-Memory} \cite{gmemory} (hierarchical memory ``nurturing the progressive evolution
of agent teams,'' i.e.\ task $N{+}1$ better because of $1{\dots}N$ across a team), plus
\textbf{RCR-Router} \cite{rcrrouter} (routing \emph{structured memory to agents within a
task}) and the \textbf{transactive-memory} framing for agents. So ``team competence
accumulates'' is \emph{not} our novelty. Two things differ. \textbf{(i)} Each peer is a
\textbf{long-lived, human-in-the-loop \emph{session identity} that lived the work} --- not an
ephemeral agent doing retrieval over a shared memory store. We are careful to state what that buys,
because our own ablations (\S V-D) constrain it: the lived identity does \emph{not} raise outcome
\emph{capability} (a fresh session with the same committed carrier reaches the same fixes); what it
buys is \textbf{efficiency and routing quality} --- fewer steps to a correct fix, and a lead who can
route work by a peer's demonstrated history rather than a declared persona. \textbf{(ii)} Routing is
therefore a \textbf{lead's grounded judgment over real artifacts} (bus, asset cards,
\texttt{CLAUDE.md}), keyed on lived history rather than a self-described role. We do \emph{not} list
``experience report vs.\ benchmark evaluation'' as a third differentiator: that is a difference in
\emph{rigour}, and in the direction of less. We frame our contribution as \emph{under-explored
relative to} that memory-augmented line, from which we differ in kind (efficiency-and-routing from
\emph{lived} identities, studied as a running deployment) --- not as a gap nobody has touched.

\section{The Architecture}
One base model; each session a persistent identity with durable memory; a read-only observer
(Conductor) that never drives the agents. Primitives, each landed as a versioned mechanism in
operation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bus} --- append-only shared log; directed mail is \textbf{auto-delivered} (idle
  recipient woken), which is the courier-elimination mechanism (and, honestly, an
  \emph{unreliable} one --- \S V-A).
\item \textbf{Verified orders} --- \texttt{deliver} reads the artifact back and refuses unless
  it landed.
\item \textbf{Project layer} --- a nominated lead drafts a job-DAG plan the human approves at
  one gate, then fans jobs (directed orders) along dependency edges; a \emph{peer}, not the
  lead, accepts the lead's own deliverables.
\item \textbf{Decision shield} --- worker decides technical calls and logs them; lead decides
  project calls; a denylist + severity hatch go straight to the human, and the lead is
  \emph{structurally barred} from deciding them.
\item \textbf{Governance} --- SHA-pinned push gate; observer-owned admission throttle (not the
  lead --- fox/henhouse); a \textbf{measured} per-project token meter (estimation is theater;
  only the live meter gates).
\end{itemize}

The through-line is \textbf{verify the outcome, not the intention.}

\section{The Method: In-Vivo Adversarial Co-Design}
The design loop that produced the above: (1) a session hits a \emph{silent-loss} failure in
real work (a tool reports success while something is quietly wrong); (2) it publishes the
specimen to the bus; (3) \textbf{context-divergent peers attack the proposed fix} --- and,
because their divergence is real (different accumulated histories), they attack from \textbf{different
empirical vantages}, which we measure and \emph{bound} in \S V-D: they are independent \emph{only
relative to} that divergence, not absolutely, so this is not self-review by one model echoing itself,
but neither do we claim uncorrelated errors in the estimator sense (\S V-D); (4) the human gates only
the irreversible.

\subsection{RQ1 --- Courier elimination (proxy, with a latency tradeoff)}
1,090 directed messages (as-of the corpus stamp) are \emph{candidates} for auto-delivery ---
hand-offs a human would otherwise relay --- so the count is a \textbf{ceiling on relays avoided},
not a claim that all were delivered: the realized auto-delivery count is a \textbf{GAP} --- it is a
runtime event the bus log does not record, so we report the ceiling the mechanism could eliminate, not
the realized figure. Indeed the mechanism
is unreliable: in this paper's own production, a directed job failed to auto-wake its peer (stale
watermark) and \textbf{the human relayed it} --- an actual delivery below the ceiling. We report the
counterexample. The correct human-touch
\textbf{instrument} is \texttt{history.jsonl} (un-swept, back to January), \emph{not}
transcripts (a 30-day cleanup horizon, \S VI) which also carry an \textbf{11.9$\times$
\texttt{tool\_result} overcount} trap.

\textbf{The longitudinal human-touch series (the method's clearest observational signal).}
From \texttt{history.jsonl} (human prompts only, un-swept back to January), we normalize touches by
\emph{active project} per month (Table~\ref{tab:touch}). \textbf{Three monthly buckets cannot
establish a trend}, and the middle is non-monotonic (June $\approx$ May), so we report the \emph{shape},
not a single ratio: prompts per active project sit near 70 across May--June, then \textbf{drop to 36 in
July}, \emph{while} concurrent projects grow from 16 to 34 (April, the substrate's build-out month, is
excluded as bootstrap-heavy, as January and March are). To check whether July's drop is a trend or a
single step, a \textbf{finer weekly breakdown} (14 weeks, artifact repo) shows the same downward
\emph{direction} --- from the $\sim$35--40 band in early May to the $\sim$13 band by late July --- so
we read it as a sustained decline, not a one-month artifact. \textbf{The weekly and monthly figures
are on different time bases and are not comparable in magnitude}: a week's prompt count (roughly a
quarter of a month's) is divided by that week's active projects, so weekly per-project figures run
lower --- the smaller numerator dominates. We use the weekly series only to confirm \emph{direction},
not to sharpen the ratio. This is the one longitudinal signal that speaks to the
\emph{method} claim rather than the compounding claim: as the substrate matures and carries more work
in parallel, the human's per-project involvement drops, which is the courier $\rightarrow$ architect
shift the method is for. We report it with the standard longitudinal confounds --- the task mix
changed, the operator learned, and per-\emph{active}-project is a proxy for per-\emph{delivered}-project
--- so it is a trend, not a controlled measurement.

\textbf{And RQ1 is a \emph{tradeoff}, not a pure win --- stated because hiding it is less
credible.} Coordination over an async bus + a lead's wake cycle can be \textbf{slower in
wall-clock than a synchronous human courier}: in this deployment a directed wake took
\textbf{minutes}, and in the counterexample above the mechanism failed outright and the human
was faster. The substrate optimizes the \textbf{human's attention} (fewer relays to run), and
can \emph{worsen} the \textbf{critical-path latency} of any single hand-off. The honest claim
is \emph{attention saved}, not \emph{time saved} --- RQ5 (\S V-E) shows the same shape at the
single-agent level (context bought steps and wall-clock but \emph{more} tokens).

\subsection{RQ2 --- Failure modes closed, with real ablations (+ the defect taxonomy)}
MEASURED: of the 38 \texttt{fix} commits (as-of the corpus stamp), $\sim$20 close \emph{named coordination failure modes}.
Several are \textbf{ablation-structured} --- disable the mechanism, the failure returns --- and
at least one ablation \emph{ran in operation}: the SHA-pinned push token, unpinned, would let
an approval for commit A push commit B; the gate \textbf{refused a stale approval live} during
this project. The two-phase-commit and member-cursor ablations are specified and pending.

\textbf{A defect taxonomy the fleet converged on (a secondary contribution).} Writing and
ablating the cases surfaced a small, reusable classification of the \emph{silent} defects the
method exists to catch, each with a \textbf{distinct} remedy --- the point being that a single
discipline (``just replicate,'' ``just tag'') sails past most of them:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single sample promoted to a property} --- a condition true of one run asserted as
  general. Remedy: \textbf{replicate + tag}. Sharpened (sizer): a \emph{measured} number
  promoted past its conditions is \emph{worse} than a derived one, because the \texttt{measured}
  badge actively defends the error.
\item \textbf{Green-but-wrong / wired-but-broken in the untested direction} --- a gate green +
  documented + shipped, passing every gate-ON test while the gate-OFF path leaks (91emulator,
  rt1180). Replaying the green measurement \emph{n} times passes \emph{n} times; the only fix
  is asserting a \textbf{different} measurement --- \textbf{execute the off-state / your own
  reproduction once}.
\item \textbf{Provenance-tier silently dropped during remediation} --- re-anchoring a number to
  a \emph{recipe} downgrades MEASURED $\rightarrow$ reproducible-in-principle until someone runs
  it (band, 91emulator). Remedy: run the recipe and \emph{say you did} (91emulator did --- the
  mutation reproduces \texttt{1,409,307,648}).
\item \textbf{Conservative-error-is-durable} --- a wrong-but-safe-looking value evades the
  provenance check that would normally catch it. ``A conservative error is not a safe error; it
  is a durable one.''
\item \textbf{Thoroughness regression} --- the context shortcut that made the agent fast made
  it skip the secondary gap (mcxn947 DISMAP; pai-sizer's third site). Remedy: re-derive under a
  differently-drawn boundary / forced first-principles trace.
\end{enumerate}

And the \textbf{remedy split} the same convergence produced: \textbf{TAG} (cheap disclosure;
catches a condition you measured but did not state) $\rightarrow$ \textbf{ABLATE} (an
invariant/control; catches a condition you \emph{believed held and did not}) $\rightarrow$
\textbf{HARVEST} (a parallel peer at the same boundary building the same artifact is a
\emph{naturally-occurring} off-state ablation --- 91emulator$\leftrightarrow$93emulator,
sizer$\leftrightarrow$pai-sizer --- cheaper than a designed one, and record-visible: cross-tree
agreement on different fixes is derived-not-copied). \emph{Shipping only the tag documents the
bug rather than fixing it.}

\subsection{RQ3 --- Defect discovery by vantage (machine-evidenced)}
We split two claims review conflated. \textbf{(i) A measurement vantage exists} (supporting,
not the RQ3 claim): MEASURED (jaws), a 38-Bash-call build had \textbf{17 measurement calls} (12
\texttt{/proc} probes, 4 runs) --- a vantage a browser assistant could not occupy, proven from
the event log. \textbf{(ii) A peer caught what the author shipped} (the actual RQ3 claim, and
it needs the \emph{same} timestamp rigor --- A ships at t0, B refutes at t1, both from the
record). We enumerate the \textbf{record-attested} cross-session catches (14 attested, 13 classifiable; tabulated in
the artifact repository) and here name four representative ones, each with \emph{attribution
corrected} against the record after review flagged two mis-attributions in earlier drafts (a
``\texttt{backend} tag-flip caught by a peer'' was in fact self-caught, and a claimed ``orb\_slam
caught four errors'' had no record --- both removed).
\textbf{image\_gen $\rightarrow$ backend}: backend's RTX-5090 idle-power \emph{denominator}
(64.97~W) was contaminated by image\_gen's own resident tenant; a clean board idles near 21~W, a
physical floor image\_gen held.
\textbf{docs $\rightarrow$ orb\_slam}: orb\_slam's ``fixed-K Amdahl overhead'' bandwidth model,
refuted by diffing two derivations on the shared tensor table (K doubles when the workload doubles,
so a fixed overhead is impossible --- the model refuted itself on its own arithmetic).
\textbf{imx95-media-test $\rightarrow$ qualcomm}: qualcomm's ``verified'' Neutron INT8 model was
quantized \emph{without calibration normalization} (wrong input scale); imx95 reproduced qualcomm's
exact numbers to five decimals, then corrected the parameter.
\textbf{holobench $\leftrightarrow$ rt1180}: holobench's ``+64~s rejoin stall'' was its own scorer's
arrival-stamp backlog --- rt1180 relocated the measurement \emph{into the guest} (a guest-emitted
timestamp holobench structurally cannot make; commit \texttt{4059f7633f}) and a subsequent scorer
replay put guest-side re-acquisition at 0.0~s. We keep the two artifacts distinct (the instrument
commit vs.\ the replayed figure), pinned t0 (stall reported) $<$ t1 (refuted).
\textbf{A record-attested instance from the ablations:} in \texttt{ablation\_sizer} a \textbf{blind,
zero-context} arm caught a \textbf{real overstatement in the author's own case} (129 defective cells
reachable via the engine API but \emph{not} the shipped UI) in nine tool calls. \textbf{Cases are illustration; the events (order claims,
timestamps, tool-call logs) are the evidence.}

\subsection{RQ4 --- Compounding competence: claim, confound, and the ablation test}
\textbf{Correlation, MEASURED:} context-carrying sessions recognized bug-classes fast (e.g.\ a
register-coverage gate green over 12/370, the class recognized in seconds because prior tasks
had named it; one-line fix $\rightarrow$ 12/370$\rightarrow$370/370, surfacing a
dead-time-zero shoot-through). \textbf{The confound (owned):} same model $\rightarrow$ this may be ``persistent
memory works,'' a known result, and memory-\emph{present} is not memory-\emph{causal}.
\textbf{The test (LANDED) --- six A/B ablations + the RQ5 baseline, and the result is a
\emph{disconfirmation} of the naive claim.} A \emph{genuinely isolated} fresh session
(harness-isolation caveat binding) vs.\ a context-carrying one on the same task, measuring
re-derivation cost:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{mahjong-together --- the best-powered arm, and the disconfirmation's backbone}
  (coach affordance overriding an engine fact; \textbf{N=8 per arm --- the only powered
  ablation of the six}): \textbf{16/16 reached the real architectural fix}; BLIND mean 5.4
  tool-calls / 37.4k tok vs PRIMED 6.5 / 44.2k --- priming bought \textbf{a small cost, no
  benefit}, and all 16 used no git history. A \textbf{clean, powered NEGATIVE}: a fresh clone
  holding the carrier re-derives the fix; session-memory adds nothing to the \emph{outcome}. We
  lead with this arm deliberately --- a disconfirmation is only as strong as its best-powered
  evidence.
\item \textbf{mcxn947 --- a COUNTER-CURRENT (color), not the foundation: N=1, post-hoc-graded,
  and its own author cautions against causal weight} (register-coverage hole; 3 cold
  clean-room arms): all three re-derived the identical one-line fix (3--5 tool calls, 46--105
  s). Beyond negative, it showed a vivid \emph{thoroughness regression} --- the
  context-carrying run pattern-matched a pre-staged answer and \textbf{stopped}, missing a
  second register class (DISMAP) the \textbf{blind} arm caught. Suggestive, but single-instance
  and unpowered; we present it as color on the powered result above, never as its base.
\item \textbf{93emulator} (idle-vs-load clock constant; \textbf{N=18}, fictional subject to
  kill harness contamination): \textbf{cost-contingent.} When measuring is
  \emph{hypothetical/costly} the standing-order arm wins unanimously (control ships the
  confidently-wrong idle value; treatment re-derives the hazard); when the measurement is made
  \textbf{cheap} (a runnable probe), \textbf{both arms 6/6 ship the right number} --- the base
  model just does it. The residual real effect is \emph{a disposition at a cost/effort fork},
  not added capability.
\item \textbf{imx95-media-test (INT8)} (INT8 class-score collapse; objective corr $>$ 0.90): the fresh
  agent reached the \textbf{same root cause in $\sim$the same $\sim$4 steps}, solved it (6 m 44
  s, 16 calls, cls-corr 0.956). PARTIAL DISCONFIRMATION --- refines the claim to a \emph{bound}
  (below).
\item \textbf{sizer} (\textbf{one of the two pre-registered ablations} --- rubric hash published to
  the bus before any agent launched; N=3/arm): tests whether a \emph{wrong} carrier actively
  \textbf{harms}. It does \textbf{not} --- stale docs had \textbf{zero measurable effect}
  (misled-to-wrong-file 0/3 in \emph{both} arms; the stale-doc arm was marginally
  \emph{cheaper}, and every agent detected the staleness unprompted). \emph{Bonus:}
  a blind zero-context arm caught a real \textbf{overstatement in the author's
  own case} in nine tool calls.
\item \textbf{imx95-media-test (C2 open-search sequel)} (\textbf{pre-registered rubric}
  --- hashed and independently witnessed before the fresh arms ran; Arm~A scored \emph{post-hoc}
  from the bus record): a distinct
  experiment from the INT8 ablation above, testing the open-search \emph{ceiling}, with the same
  A/B shape --- \textbf{Arm~A} the context-carrying session (scored post-hoc from record: it produced
  the correct empirical rule immediately on the bus, R1--R4 satisfied at $\approx$one message) vs.\
  \textbf{Arm~B} four fresh isolated agents run under the hashed rubric. Its symmetric
  falsifier fired --- all four fresh arms \emph{also} solved the constructed task at floor cost, so it
  was secretly bisectable --- and it is reported as a \textbf{NULL} (no capability gap observed, but
  no clean ceiling measurement either), with the finding \emph{why} (reproducibility
  $\perp$ open-search; detailed below).
\item \textbf{RQ5} (\S V-E, the baseline, not an ablation): both arms correct with zero help; context bought 2.6$\times$
  wall-clock and 36\% fewer steps but \textbf{more} tokens.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The result, stated plainly: the committed carrier is what compounds; lived context buys
efficiency and recognition, not capability.} This is a powered negative that corrects a widely-held
intuition, and we state it without hedge. A fresh clone \emph{holding the repo} re-derives the fix
because \textbf{what compounds is what is still true in the carrier when the next session reads it},
and the \textbf{executable} carrier governs
(code / assert / invariant); a \emph{prose} carrier is ``neither the asset nor the liability
anyone claimed'' (sizer's wrong-doc ablation is self-refuting because the code is there to
check it against). \textbf{The efficiency/capability line, made operational (so ``efficiency''
is falsifiable, not a relabel that absorbs every gain):} we score an ablation arm as a
\textbf{capability} gain iff the context-carrying arm \emph{reaches a correct fix the fresh arm
does NOT}, and as an \textbf{efficiency} gain iff it reaches the \emph{same} correct fix
\emph{more cheaply} (fewer steps/tokens/time). Under that test the \textbf{five floor ablations} (the five non-C2 ablations, all testing the
crisp-symptom floor) \textbf{+ RQ5 are efficiency, not capability} --- across every arm the fresh agent reached a correct fix
(mahjong 16/16, mcxn947 3/3, 93emulator 6/6, INT8 in $\sim$4 steps, sizer 3/3 correct in
\emph{both} arms (0/3 misled), both RQ5 arms), so the measured advantages (RQ5's 2.6$\times$ fewer steps;
mahjong's flat outcome) are cheaper-path, not can-vs-cannot. \textbf{C2, the sixth ablation, is
excluded from this verdict on purpose:} both its arms reached the answer, but its symmetric
falsifier fired (the task was not genuinely open-search), so C2 yields \emph{no}
capability-vs-efficiency signal about the ceiling --- it is a pre-registered NULL (below), not a
data point in the efficiency finding. \emph{This is a falsifiable claim: a single
ablation where the fresh arm FAILS and the context arm succeeds would move it to
``capability'' --- we looked and did not find one on the tasks tested.} The
\textbf{defensible, bounded RQ4b claim:} accumulated context compounds \textbf{most on an open
search a fresh agent cannot cheaply bisect} and \textbf{least on a crisp symptom with an
objective oracle} (any agent bisects fast); on some specimens context even shows a
\textbf{thoroughness regression} (pattern-match-and-stop). \textbf{This bound is half-measured:
the crisp-symptom FLOOR is MEASURED (the five floor ablations bisected fast, and even C2's ceiling
attempt collapsed to a fast bisection); the open-search CEILING
resists measurement --- and we report the \emph{pre-registered sequel that tried to measure it
and why it could not.}} A fresh attempt (imx95-media-test, C2 --- rubric hashed and bus-published +
independently witnessed BEFORE any arm ran) built a ``hard'' open-search task in a sterile,
reproducible sandbox; its pre-registered symmetric falsifier fired: all 4 fresh arms reached
the correct answer at floor cost ($\sim$4 tool calls, $\sim$60 s), so the task was secretly
\emph{bisectable}, and per the pre-registration it is reported as a NULL --- NOT as support for
the floor. The finding is the reason: reproducibility and the open-search property are in
TENSION --- a sterile, offline, pre-registerable task must place its answer in an inspectable
artifact, and the instant the answer is in an artifact, \texttt{strings} turns the task into a
bisection. Real open-search cost lives in the answer being \emph{absent from every artifact}
(learnable only by iterating on hardware over days --- the imx95 converter case). So the
ceiling is HARD TO INSTRUMENT, which is a \emph{different} claim from being ABSENT; the honest
earning test is a \textbf{field A/B on real hardware (natural-history, controlled where
possible), not a sandbox} --- and a contrived ``open'' task that looked bisectable would have
manufactured a \emph{false null on the floor}, which the pre-registered symmetric falsifier
exists to prevent, and did. We report the naive ``memory makes task \emph{N+1} dramatically
cheaper'' as \textbf{refuted on the tasks tested}, and the open-search ceiling as an
\textbf{honest, reasoned GAP} --- measurable floor, un-sandboxable ceiling --- rather than a
claim dressed as a result.

\textbf{A discipline the fleet imposed on us, and it is the sharper point:} the C2 null and
\texttt{sizer}'s stale-doc null (\S V-B/RQ2) are \textbf{NOT independent corroboration --- they
are one mechanism on two surfaces.} Both tasks were \emph{bisectable by construction because
the ground truth was inspectable in the artifacts}: sizer's arms scored 0/3-misled because the
\textbf{code} was there to check the doc against; C2's arms scored 4/4-solved because the
\textbf{answer} was \texttt{strings}-able out of the model file. By the convergence-provenance
rule (\S V-D independence), this is \textbf{common-dependency at the mechanism tier} (and
shared-base-model) --- near-zero independent weight. So we cite them as \textbf{two
illustrations of one constraint (reproducibility $\perp$ open-search), never as two nulls
agreeing.} What \emph{is} load-bearing is the \textbf{constraint itself}: it held across two
domains (stale-doc/app vs converter/NPU) and two opposite failure directions (0/3 vs 4/4),
which is evidence the constraint is real --- \emph{even though the two nulls do not
independently corroborate it.} \textbf{The finding generalizes; the data points do not
replicate} --- stated exactly so a reviewer cannot read ``two nulls'' as ``replicated.''
\emph{(Sequel artifacts, remote-verified 2026-07-27: pre-registration
\texttt{imx95-media-test@abeab3dc64cb}, results \texttt{@d35a64bf6d3a}, on
github.com/kylefoxaustin/imx95-media-test.)} \textbf{Honest asymmetry:} two of the six
ablations pre-registered their rubric (\texttt{sizer} and the C2 sequel); the other four are
honestly-run but \emph{post-hoc graded} (\S VI).

\textbf{The independence sub-claim (RQ4-adjacent) --- the measured bound, at exactly the strength the
record supports.} A skeptic rightly objects that our divergence proxies (disjoint task histories, low
memory-file overlap, divergent tool-call sequences) measure divergence of \emph{inputs}, whereas
independence of estimators requires uncorrelated \emph{errors} --- and two same-model sessions share a
generative prior, so their reasoning errors can correlate even with different inputs. We tested this
directly rather than waving at it. We classified the \textbf{13 solid record-attested cross-session
catches} (\S V-C; a 14th is an incidental exposure, excluded) as \textbf{factual/local} (a discrete
fact the catcher held differently) vs.\ \textbf{reasoning-shaped} (the catcher reasoned to a
different conclusion). The split is \textbf{6 factual, 7 reasoning-shaped} --- $\approx$1:1, which
\emph{at face value would refute} an ``input-divergence-only'' reading. But the honest reading is narrower, and it is the one
the record supports: \textbf{every reasoning-shaped catch was made by a peer at a different
\emph{empirical vantage}} --- a measurement relocated into the subject (a guest-emitted clock),
different physical silicon, or the ship-vs-implement test-direction asymmetry --- \textbf{not by an
independent reasoner spotting a co-located reasoning flaw from identical inputs}. The corpus authors
say so themselves (``a different vantage point, a property of the substrate, not of any one model
being smarter''), and \textbf{there is no instance in the record of a peer catching a same-input
reasoning error.} We therefore claim independence \emph{only relative to} the divergence proxies,
never absolutely: peers do catch reasoning-shaped defects, but they catch them by being
\emph{empirically positioned} differently --- the input/grounding divergence the proxies measure ---
which is a measured bound on the claim, not evidence of uncorrelated errors in the estimator sense.
\subsection{RQ5 --- Baseline: cold orchestrator vs.\ peer substrate (pre-registered)}
One matched task, run both ways, \textbf{pre-registered} (frozen spec + black-box acceptance
test + directional prediction committed before either arm ran; the instrument was first proven
satisfiable via a throwaway reference impl, then proven to actually test the feature against
the unmodified baseline). Task: add \texttt{project pause/resume} to the $\sim$700-line
\texttt{project.sh} state machine --- a \emph{new instance of a familiar class}, objectively
gradeable on six behaviors. \textbf{Arm A} = a genuinely memoryless fresh session (separate
\texttt{\$HOME}, no memory/bus/hooks, verified at run time). \textbf{Arm B} = \texttt{backend},
the peer with the most lived \texttt{project.sh} history, business-as-usual (not told it was an
experiment). Both worked from an identical repo copy that \emph{includes} \texttt{CLAUDE.md} (a
real clone has it --- the conservative choice, it helps Arm A) but excludes the spec and
\texttt{.git}.

\textbf{MEASURED result --- and it does not simply flatter the thesis.} Both arms reached a
\emph{functionally correct} implementation (all six behaviors) with \textbf{zero substantive
human help}. So a fair clone \emph{holding the committed carrier} is fully capable with no
session-memory --- \textbf{corroborating the ablation convergence in \S V-D.} But lived context
bought real \textbf{efficiency}: Arm B finished in \textbf{7.8 min vs 20.1 (2.6$\times$), with
36\% fewer tool calls (14 vs 22) and 41\% fewer lines added (39 vs 66 --- more surgical)} --- it
\emph{recognized} the state machine rather than exploring it. The honest counter-current,
reported: Arm B used \textbf{38\% \emph{more} output tokens (35.2k vs 25.6k)} --- the
compounding benefit here is \textbf{speed and recognition, not token cost.} The pre-registered
prediction (B fewer human turns + less wall-clock) was \textbf{confirmed on wall-clock/steps,
tied at zero on human turns} (the task was tractable enough that neither needed intervention
--- so RQ5 measures the single-agent efficiency delta, \emph{not} the courier delta of RQ1).
\emph{Bonus, in-apparatus:} the \textbf{frozen} acceptance test carried a latent defect (a
crude substring assert tripping on a correct ``NOT dispatched'' refusal); it was
\textbf{disclosed, not patched} --- editing a frozen instrument post-hoc is the exact sin this
paper studies (\S V-F). Full protocol + both arms' diffs: \texttt{cases\_rq5-baseline.md}.

\subsection{The paper as an instrument (an operational finding, not a controlled result)}
Asking sessions to substantiate claims \emph{to an external audience} forced verification of
things taken on trust internally. In git: \texttt{sizer} found a \textbf{46-day defect} (129 cells
rendering wrong fps, count never zero, never visible) while writing its case --- after 46 days of
use/test/review found nothing; \texttt{pai-sizer} surfaced a validation-across-an-unsafe-version
provenance defect the same way. We state the \textbf{hit-rate}: of \textbf{19} case-writing sessions,
\textbf{4 ($\sim$21\%)} reported a correction surfaced during the writing (\texttt{sizer},
\texttt{pai-sizer}, \texttt{tipometer}, the RQ5 apparatus's own frozen-test defect); the rest
reported none. We are careful about what this is and is not. \textbf{It has no control arm:} we did
not measure how many defects surface in 19 sessions of \emph{equivalent effort spent not writing for
an external audience}, so 21\% has nothing to beat, and the underlying effect --- careful explanation
to an outside reader surfaces latent defects --- is well established for design documents and code
review; \emph{the only novel element here is that the writers were agents}. And it is not fully
``testimony-free'': the \emph{commits} are machine evidence, but the \emph{attribution} (``the defect
was surfaced by the act of writing'') is a causal judgment made and reported by the same sessions ---
two different epistemic objects, which we keep separate. So we report it as an \textbf{unanticipated
operational finding with a stated hit-rate and no control arm} --- worth recording, not a headline
result.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Self-evidencing circularity.} A corpus of first-person cases
  about coordination, curated for a paper about coordination, is self-report at scale.
  Mitigation: cases are demoted to illustration; every load-bearing claim rests on the
  mechanical record.
\item \textbf{Single-model confound.} Divergence is memory/context, not different minds; we own
  the composition and do not claim emergence.
\item \textbf{Selection bias / no denominator.} The open call collects confirming specimens by
  construction. We hold $\geq$1 deliberate counter-case (a bug recognized instantly but
  re-tripped twice --- recognition cost $\rightarrow$0, prevention cost unchanged), frame the
  corpus as illustrative, and note the missing denominator explicitly.
\item \textbf{30-day record horizon.} The platform silently deleted transcripts $>$30 days (now
  disabled); the pre-June transcript record is GAP. Longitudinal claims use \texttt{history.jsonl}
  + git, which survive.
\item \textbf{Ablation methodology.} (a) \emph{Grading
  asymmetry} --- two of six ablations (\texttt{sizer} and the C2 sequel) published a rubric hash
  \emph{before} results; the other four are honestly-run but \textbf{post-hoc graded}, so
  retrofitting is disavowed, not detectable. \texttt{sizer} is the \emph{only} ablation whose
  \textbf{both} arms ran under a pre-published rubric; C2's pre-registration covers its rubric and
  its fresh (Arm~B) runs, but its Arm~A was scored post-hoc from record ((c) below), so C2 sits
  partly in both categories. (b) \emph{Isolation is a claim, not a given} --- the
  harness pre-injects a fresh agent's cwd, git status, and memory \emph{index}, so ``blind'' arms
  are mitigated, not sterile; the cleanest arms used \textbf{fictional subjects} (93emulator) or
  audited each agent's actual tool calls. (c) \emph{Historical arms are not re-runnable} --- some
  context-carrying arms (the INT8 ablation's Arm~A and C2's Arm~A) are transcripts of past work
  (the answer already known), so they bound
  rather than measure. (d) \emph{N is small} (1--8/arm) and outcomes are often categorical (no
  variance). Each is stated in the individual \texttt{ablation\_*.md}.
\item \textbf{$N{=}1$, one operator, one workstation, one model family.}
\item \textbf{Self-authorship}, including \emph{this reframe} --- written by an interested lead,
  corrected by peers; we prefer machine evidence throughout and mark recollection RECALLED.
\item \textbf{The bystander-catch corpus contracted under audit.} While preparing this paper, two
  claimed cross-session catches did not survive record-checking --- a ``\texttt{backend} tag-flip
  caught by a peer'' was in fact self-caught, and ``orb\_slam caught four independent errors'' had no
  case-file or bus record --- and both were removed (\S V-C). We report the contraction rather than
  quietly correcting it, because it is itself evidence for the provenance discipline the paper claims:
  the corpus is what the record supports (14 attested, 13 classifiable), not what narration remembered.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work: A Pre-Registered Replication}
The paper's central threat is $N{=}1$ --- one domain (systems/tooling), one operator, one
workstation, one model family. We therefore \emph{pre-register} the replication that would
falsify or corroborate the claims, and state its prediction before running it (predict-then-run,
a discipline the fleet itself enforces):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Vary the confounding axes, not the surface.} Repeat the experiment on a
  \textbf{structurally different domain --- ideally not software} (e.g.\ scientific literature
  synthesis, hardware/product design, operations, or creative work), where ``a task'' and
  ``expertise'' mean something different in kind. Ideally with a \textbf{different human
  architect}, to remove the ``maybe it is this operator'' confound.
\item \textbf{Hold the \emph{method} constant, vary the \emph{content}.} The claim under test is
  not ``the tool runs on another codebase'' but that \emph{the composition} --- persistent
  context-heavy peers + a shared bus + verified orders + a human gating irreversibility ---
  reproduces the signatures: \textbf{compounding competence} (a measured downward per-task-cost
  trajectory via the same ablation protocol), \textbf{peer review catching the lead's blind
  spot}, and the \textbf{self-designed substrate}.
\item \textbf{Prediction (falsifiable):} if the principles are real and not artifacts of this
  domain/operator, the \emph{same} signatures appear on the new ``thing''; if they are artifacts,
  they do not. A null result is as informative as a positive one and will be reported as such.
\item \textbf{Feasibility:} the replication needs no new system --- only a new \emph{goal}
  pointed at a fleet (\texttt{project new <thing>}), which is precisely what makes the method's
  claim to generality testable rather than rhetorical.
\end{itemize}

This is the sequel experiment; naming it here is the honest acknowledgement that a single
deployment, however rich, establishes a \emph{method worth replicating}, not a law.

\section{Conclusion}
Conductor is an experience report on a specific, nameable composition --- one model, durable
per-context memory, a shared bus, a human-as-architect gate calibrated to reversibility ---
hardened \emph{in vivo} by its own context-divergent peers. Its most defensible results are on
the mechanical record: a load-sharing division of labour (many peers, a measured head --- Gini
0.693 --- but no dominant orchestrator), named failure modes closed with at least one live
ablation, and machine-evidenced bystander discovery (a cross-session catch corpus of 14 attested / 13 classifiable, audited and
bounded in \S V-D). The paper-as-instrument effect (\S V-F) is reported separately as an operational
finding with a stated hit-rate and no control arm, not among these. Its headline intuition ---
compounding competence ---
was \textbf{put to the test} (six A/B ablations + a pre-registered baseline) and the naive form
was \textbf{disconfirmed}: what compounds is the \emph{committed carrier}, and lived context
buys \emph{efficiency and recognition}, not raw capability, most on open searches a fresh agent
cannot cheaply bisect. We report that sharpened, partly-negative result in full --- it is a
stronger paper for surviving its own test rather than dodging it, and the process that produced
this honesty (peers refuting the lead over the bus, an ablation refuting its own author's case)
\textbf{is the very mechanism under study.}

\textbf{The one contribution to leave with} (honesty is not thinness): the reusable result is
\emph{the method} --- \textbf{in-vivo adversarial co-design of a coordination substrate by
persistent, context-divergent peers under a human gating only irreversibility} --- offered as an
existence proof with a mechanism, not a validated superiority. Its cleanest reflexive demonstration
is the paper's own review, and it is \emph{evidenced, not testimonial}, because it instantiates the
very vantage bound of \S V-D one level up. The fleet's internal panel occupied the \emph{deployment}
vantage --- it could see the substrate, the commits, the ablations --- and caught \textbf{substance}:
a circularity, a single-model confound, unrun ablations. It \emph{missed}, across five internal
revisions, the classes it was structurally positioned not to see --- \textbf{cross-section
consistency} (a figure disavowed in one section and featured in another), \textbf{arithmetic} (three
mismatches in a recompute), \textbf{missing prior art}, and a \textbf{venue-compliance} defect (an AI
entity in the author block). Independent external review, occupying the \emph{submission} vantage,
caught precisely those. \textbf{Each of these defects, and the vantage that caught it, is resolvable}:
the numbered draft series (\texttt{draft-v1}--\texttt{v5.md}) and the submitted
\texttt{conductor-paper.tex} itself, the internal panel reviews, and the external review notes
(\texttt{review\_external.md}, which records each round) are all archived and pushed in the artifact
repository, and the git history of the paper shows when each defect entered and was fixed --- the
internal-panel catches in the draft series, the external-review catches (the disavowed-then-featured
figure, the arithmetic, the prior art, the author-block entity) in the post-v5 \texttt{.tex}
revisions --- so this is not testimony, it is checkable in minutes.
(By the paper's own rule, a claim a reviewer cannot resolve is not yet evidence; this one now can be.)
Same mechanism as \S V-D --- a reviewer catches what its vantage exposes, and
misses what it does not --- with this paper as the specimen: not ``the panel made it sharper,'' but a
measured account of \emph{which vantage caught which class of defect}. The compounding intuition is
the \emph{motivating hypothesis we tested and bounded}, not the contribution; the method, demonstrated
on itself --- including on the limits of its own review --- is.

\textbf{A transferable secondary contribution} the deployment produced, and one we think travels
beyond this setting: a \emph{non-independence discipline for corroboration}. When two of the fleet's
own null results (the C2 open-search null and \texttt{sizer}'s stale-doc null) agreed, the fleet
declined to count them as replication --- they share a mechanism (both tasks were bisectable because
their ground truth was inspectable), so by a common-dependency rule they carry near-zero independent
weight. The rule --- \emph{before crediting two results as corroborating, check whether they share a
generative mechanism, and if so discount them} --- is exactly what most authors would have banked as
replication, and it applies to any convergence among same-family estimators (\S V-D).

One last observation the deployment forced on us, because it kept mistaking a rung for the
summit: a result is not durable until it is \emph{reachable by a reviewer}, and the fleet
walked that ladder three times in a day --- \textbf{untracked file $\rightarrow$
tracked-but-local commit $\rightarrow$ pushed to a remote} --- each rung fixing a different
failure mode (silent edit $\rightarrow$ machine loss $\rightarrow$ un-verifiability), and
\emph{each one felt like the end of the job} (pai-sizer, on the C2 sequel's own artifacts).
``Committed'' is not a synonym for ``durable,'' and neither is ``tracked.'' We state it because
this paper's evidence \emph{is} those commits, and a claim a reviewer cannot resolve is, at that
moment, not yet evidence.
